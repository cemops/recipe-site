Tonkotsu Ramen


Ingredients

Tonkotsu Ramen:

8 Cups Tonkotsu Pork Broth (See Recipe)
12 oz Good Quality Dried Ramen Noodles
4 Large or Extra Large Eggs
2 - 3 oz Enoki or other Mushrooms ( I used oyster last time)
Thinly Sliced Green Onions

Chashu Pork Belly
2 lb Pork Belly - rolled and tied
1/4 Cup Soy Sauce
1/2 Cup Sake
1/2 Cup Mirin
1/4 Cup Sugar
2 - 3 Cloves Garlic - left whole
2 Green Onions - coursely chopped

Soy Bacon Tare
2 Slices Bacon
1/4 Cup Soy Sauce
2 Tbs Mirin
2 Tbs Sake
4 Tbs Shiro Miso

Miso Tare
1/2 Cup Shiro Miso
1/4 Cup Sake
1/4 Cup Mirin
Pinch Shichimi Togarashi - a Japanese Spice (optional)
1 - 2 tps Kosher Salt - depending on how salty you like your ramen

Instructions

Chashu Pork Belly

Combine soy, sake, mirin, sugar, garlic and green onions in a zip lock bag big enough to hold the pork belly. Stick a straw in the bag and seal the ziplock bag up against it. Suck out as much of the air as you can and seal
Sous vide the pork for 10 - 11 hours at 170 degrees
Remove pork from the bag, Discard the bag and marinade.
Let the chashu pork belly cool completely.
Slice across the chashu pork (so you get bacon like slices) - into 8 - 12 slices about 1/8 to 3/16 inches thick. Reserve. You won't need all of the pork for 4 servings.

Soy Bacon Tare
Combine all the ingredients in a small sauce pan. Simmer at the lowest setting for about an hour. Top up with a bit of chicken stock if needed.
Remove the bacon

Miso Tare
Combine all ingredients in a small sauce pan and simmer at the lowest setting for about 5 minutes

Medium Boiled Eggs
Bring enough water to cover the eggs to a boil. Prick the egg. Boil large eggs for 6 min & 30 seconds.
Submerge the eggs in ice water to chill. Peel and cut in half right before you serve the ramen

Assemble the Tonkotsu Ramen
Boil the ramen noodles in plenty of water as directed, usually 4 min. You don't need to salt the water.
Cook the mushrooms alongside the noodles - you just want them softened
Gently fry the chashu pork in a non - stick skillet until lightly browned
Place 1/4 of the tare in the bottom of 4 bowls
Ladle in 1/2 cup of the tonkotsu broth into each of the bowls and stir to mix.
Add the noodles. Pour in another 1 1/2 cups of broth per bowl
Top with the egg, mushrooms, pork and green onions

NOTES
Use only 1 of the tare recipes or use your own.
If you use the miso tare, you can salt your broth directly. That is easier than guessing how salty you want your tare.
If you aren't up for making chashu pork you can substitute roasted pork shoulder. It would be an awesome us of a leftover pork roast.
